# Film grammar for YouTube — Hillier-Smith-informed crossover

**Keep the YouTube audience promise**, but borrow cinema's emotional language when footage supports it.

1. Hook through POV: reveal what the creator sees / wants, then preserve a truthful reaction. Don't merely add a montage of intense shots.
2. Conflict and change: show the creator's real misunderstanding, obstacle, expert explanation or changed viewpoint, if source evidence supports it. Hillier-Smith's public 2025 analysis demonstrates this approach; the specific three-act/Dunning–Kruger arc is an illustrative option, **not a formula every creator must fit**.
3. Make pacing change with dramatic purpose. A quick explainer can transition to a still response, then to a clear revelation; don't force a regular 'dopamine spike'.
4. Audio bridges and environmental sound can create immersion across B-roll and narration. Do not replace authentic field sound with generic corporate music without reason.
5. Titles, maps and kinetic words are for necessary context/proof. YouTube cinematic ≠ 'remove all text' and film grammar ≠ 'slow down all cuts'.
6. Context matters: a travel documentary may prioritize ethics, chronology and personal reactions; a science explainer should keep demonstrations fully comprehensible.
7. Don't assume one 8-second audition can validate a multi-minute transformation. Require full arc setup/change/reaction/payoff context and actual authorized native media.
8. If `platform=youtube`, `visual_language=cinematic`, `narrative=tutorial`, **tutorial requirements still win for demonstrating steps**. The cinematic axis affects presentation, not whether to show required content.

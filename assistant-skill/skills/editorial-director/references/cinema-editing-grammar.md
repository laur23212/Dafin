# Cinema editing grammar — on-demand creative reference (v0.8.0)

These are questions and options, not mandatory film laws. Sources: `research/cinema_principles.json (source-linked, original interpretations)`.

## Decision priority
- Emotion, story, rhythm, viewer eye trace, screen plane, spatial continuity: the *relative decision hierarchy* proposed by Walter Murch. His percentages are rhetorical, **not numeric weights to compute**.
- Cut when a change of intention, information, perspective, physical action or emotional state motivates it. The right cut can be quick or very slow. Never force 3–9s per shot just because the style JSON contains a descriptive interval.
- Inspect emotional transitions: if a performer moves from neutral to grief across a cut, do not skip the transition unless omission is intentional and meaningful. Preserve genuine source context.
- Point-of-view grammar: orientation -> what subject observes -> genuine response. Kuleshov juxtapositions shape the inferred meaning. **Do not use a reaction from an unrelated time to manufacture motives, consent, emotions or causality**.
- Eye trace and continuity: make action legible with screen direction, eyelines, shot sizes and cut-on-action where useful. Disorientation is allowable only if it serves a clear narrative objective.
- Close-ups are not default filler; reserve them for detail, emotional change or new information. Wide shots can also carry strong emotional information.
- Action: show setup, direction, cause, impact and consequence; an avalanche of cuts can hide the event rather than make it exciting.
- Suspense can rely on anticipation and what is known or withheld; dramatic surprise can be sudden. Do not create a misleading factual claim to induce suspense.
- Sound belongs to the edit: use realistic ambience, J/L cuts, pre-lap dialogue, diegetic cues, sonic perspective and purposeful silence. Use only genuine handles and maintain speaker identity.
- Match cuts, dissolves, montage and hard cuts are available *choices*, never automatic rules; a visual rhyme may suggest an idea the source material never supports, so review its implications.

## Cinema is not a LUT
Do not apply fixed black bars, teal/orange color, 24fps conversion, grain, extreme shallow-DOF blur or slow motion as mandatory 'cinematic' decoration. Color should be internally consistent and serve setting, skin tones and intention. Preserve project frame rate and reframing choices unless requested.

## Review evidence
- Actual frame sequence for eye trace, spatial direction, reaction and motion; a contact sheet alone misses motion rhythm.
- Audio around both sides of a cut for sonic continuity and silence.
- Context before/after a beat to judge emotion and narrative meaning.
- If no native media is available, report *candidate diagnosis / unverified*; never claim editorial quality was observed.

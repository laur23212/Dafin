# Cinema attention, suspense, viewpoint — evidence-first notes (v0.8.1)

**Use only when the scene benefits from these checks.** A timecode is not observed footage. Ask for a revision-bound playback or frame/audio excerpts via Dafin's existing authorized tools.

- **Attention continuity:** identify focus before/after each important cut, and cues (gaze, sound, motion, narrative question). A direction reversal is not automatically a 180-degree-rule violation.
- **Perspective and Kuleshov implication:** when a viewer sees a look, a subject and a reaction, confirm chronology and context. Never infer real person's sentiment from an out-of-context juxtaposition.
- **Suspense versus surprise:** for suspense, the viewer needs enough information to expect stakes; surprise can omit a prior setup. Do not invent facts or claim a delayed reveal guarantees retention.
- **Composition and internal motion:** movement within a shot can replace a cut; watch temporal playback to see blocking and competing motion, not just a beauty frame.
- **Emotion-first editing:** Murch's ordering is a practical priority list, not numeric machine-learned weights. Do not automatically sacrifice genuine performance for a smoother match cut.
- **Sound:** consider authentic J/L bridges and silence only when source handles and rights exist.
- **Review:** propose a smallest correction, compare muted-score and full-score locally when authorized, and preserve deliberate discontinuity.

# Motion typography and readability (original guidance)

## For fast YouTube edits
- Use readable *phrases* rather than animating every word indiscriminately. Emphasize meaning-bearing words and change treatment when the semantic role changes.
- Kinetic motion needs a clear entrance, a fully readable interval, and a purposeful exit. A one-frame settle does not create readability.
- For a 2-second title, start by reserving most of the duration for stable text. A short entrance (~0.1–0.2 s) and a short exit (~0.1–0.2 s) may work **when the actual copy length and frame size support it**. These are exploratory examples, not universal limits.
- Avoid stacking a moving title, animated background caption, particles, and camera zoom in the same focal region.
- Mobile readability must be checked at export dimensions and expected player size; normalized margins are not proof of legibility.

## For cinematic edits
- Prefer subtle movement, deliberate placement and typographic restraint. Slow fades are not always needed; a clean cut to a title can be stronger.
- Font choice should match the era, tone, camera language and available contrast. Don't assume a serif always means cinematic.

## For tutorials and interviews
- Keep UI labels and on-screen steps stable while the viewer needs to act.
- Don't cover faces, hands, controls or subtitles with decorative motion.
- Respect language boundaries when splitting captions; characters per second alone cannot measure comprehension.

## Actual testing
Review entrance, the first fully settled frame, the last readable frame, and exit. Compare at realistic mobile and desktop sizes. A preview timestamp derived from metadata is only a *request* until a real frame is captured.

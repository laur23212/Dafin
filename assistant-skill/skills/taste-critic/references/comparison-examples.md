# Synthetic good-versus-weak editorial comparisons

**These are original, imaginary examples for teaching critique. They are not footage, expert-labelled data, or measured audience preferences.** A reviewer must inspect real rendered video before applying a judgment to the user's project.

## 1 — Creator YouTube: a new idea every ~2 seconds
- Context: a presenter demonstrates why an autofocus feature fails, with a clear 4-second live example.
- **Weak candidate:** Cuts away at exactly 2.0 s to a decorative B-roll clip, concealing the second half of the autofocus failure.
- **Stronger candidate:** Holds the 4-second experiment, adds a small unobtrusive failure label at the moment the subject shifts focus, then cuts to the explanation.
- **Why:** The demo itself is the visual change and proof. Tempo should serve comprehension rather than impose a two-second grid.

## 2 — YouTube kinetic title
- Context: 2-second overlay, sentence "THIS IS EVERYTHING YOU NEED TO KNOW".
- **Weak candidate:** Each of the 7 words pops with 0.25-second stagger; last word never settles before exit.
- **Stronger candidate:** Condense to "THE KEY DIFFERENCE", animate one meaningful word briefly, keep the phrase still long enough to read.
- **Why:** Hierarchy and readable hold outrank animation density.

## 3 — Cinematic travel film
- Context: quiet sunrise and long take after a difficult climb.
- **Weak candidate:** Add a whoosh, whip pan and glow title because the static shot looks "slow".
- **Stronger candidate:** Preserve landscape sound, delay or remove title, use a cut only when the feeling changes.
- **Why:** Silence and stillness may be the emotional payoff.

## 4 — Tutorial demonstration
- Context: user needs to locate a tiny toolbar icon and click it.
- **Weak candidate:** Crop and zoom every two seconds, hiding the control just before the click.
- **Stronger candidate:** Hold a stable close crop, add a precise arrow or highlighted bounding box, release the hold only after the click is complete.
- **Why:** Action continuity and comprehension dominate pacing heuristics.

## 5 — Interview documentary
- Context: speaker pauses before acknowledging a difficult fact.
- **Weak candidate:** Delete the pause and cover the statement with unrelated reaction B-roll.
- **Stronger candidate:** Preserve the relevant pause and genuine reaction, use only truthful contextual imagery.
- **Why:** Performance, honesty and emotional context cannot be optimized by silence removal alone.

## 6 — Gaming montage
- Context: user lands a decisive in-game hit.
- **Weak candidate:** Impact SFX and artificial shake play on each music beat independently of gameplay.
- **Stronger candidate:** Reserve one clear accent for the actual hit, preserving the original gameplay sound and reaction.
- **Why:** Motive and timing matter more than loudness.

## Review prompt
For each candidate, ask: what is the intended *viewer experience*? What evidence has actually been seen? Which specific change reduces confusion or increases emotional impact? What is the simplest defensible alternative? Avoid confidently rating aesthetics from textual metadata alone.
